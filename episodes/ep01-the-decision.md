# Episode 01 / Chapter 01: To Cook the World, I Have to Start With Home

> **Core Thesis (Non-Fiction & Video)**: Before I can travel the world through other people's kitchens, I need to truly understand the food that made me. And it turns out, Filipino cuisine—shaped by Indigenous, Chinese, Malay, Spanish, and American history—is already a map of the world on a single plate.

## 2. Dual-Track Structure: Essay vs. Video

### Track A: The Written Non-Fiction Essay / Chapter 1
- **Opening Scene**: A sensory memory of food in the Philippines contrasted with standing in a Melbourne supermarket or Asian grocer today—realizing that being a "decent home cook" isn't the same as truly knowing the soul and technique of your own heritage.
- **The Central Question**: Why do so many of us look outward to French, Italian, or Japanese technique to become "better cooks," while treating our own childhood food as an afterthought?
- **The Realization**: Filipino food isn't isolated—it's one of the original global fusion cuisines (Austronesian souring techniques, Hokkien wok & noodle craft, Spanish *sofrito*/*guisado*, Mexican-Manila galleon trade ingredients like tomatoes, corn, and cacao). Mastering it is the ultimate launchpad to cooking the world.

### Track B: The YouTube Video Flow (8–12 mins)

#### Hook (0:00 – 0:50)
- **Visual**: Cold open on sizzling garlic/onions or pouring vinegar into a hot pan, cutting to Melbourne streetscapes (trams, Queen Victoria Market or Footscray market) and then back to your kitchen.
- **Voiceover / A-Roll**:
  - *"I was born in the Philippines, I live in Melbourne, and I want to travel the world—not just by booking flights, but by learning how to cook."*
  - *"I'm an okay home cook. I can follow a recipe. But before I try to cook my way through Italy, Japan, or Mexico, I realized something uncomfortable: I haven't even mastered the foundational dishes of where I came from."*

#### Act I: The Ingredient Hunt in Melbourne (0:50 – 3:00)
- Visiting a Filipino/Asian grocer and local market in Melbourne.
- Introducing the **foundational flavor pillars of Philippine cooking** (sour/acid from vinegar & tamarind/calamansi, salty umami from soy sauce/fish sauce/*bagoong*, aromatics of garlic/onion/ginger/bay leaf).

#### Act II: Going Deeper Than a Recipe (3:00 – 7:30)
- Cooking the **First Foundational Dish** (e.g., *Adobo* Done Three Ways or *Adobo* vs. *Sinigang*—pushing past "how I casually make it on a Tuesday" to elevating the technique: browning, emulsion, balancing acid and fat).
- Weaving in the personal story: What this dish meant growing up in the Philippines vs. what it means cooking it as an Australian citizen in Melbourne.

#### Act III: The Map Ahead (7:30 – End)
- Tasting the finished dish and grading yourself honestly on what worked and what still needs mastery.
- **Opening the Series Loop**: Showing a notebook or board of the foundational Philippine dishes to master first, and how each one connects to the wider world (and Melbourne's multicultural kitchens) in future episodes.

## 3. Foundational Philippine Dishes Roadmap (Phase 1 Candidates)
Which dish (or pair of dishes) should anchor Episode 1, and which ones form the rest of Phase 1?
1. **Adobo** *(The Indigenous Technique)* — Mastering the alchemy of vinegar, garlic, fat rendering, and sauce emulsion.
2. **Sinigang** *(The Art of Sourness)* — Understanding acid as a primary flavor enhancer and regional souring agents.
3. **Kare-Kare & Bagoong** *(Texture, Patience & Fermentation)* — Toasted rice/peanut thickening, oxtail braising, and fermented shrimp paste.
4. **Pancit / Lumpia** *(The Chinese-Filipino Heritage)* — Wok *hei*, noodle hydration, and wrapper mastery.
5. **Caldereta / Menudo / Arroz Caldo** *(The Spanish & Galleon Trade Connection)* — The *guisado* base (garlic, onion, tomato) that bridges Filipino cooking to Spain and Latin America.
6. **Kinilaw** *(Ceviche's Older Austronesian Cousin)* — Curing raw seafood with vinegar and citrus, connecting the Philippines to the Pacific and Peru.
